# Outcome-based Product Roadmap

A product roadmap communicates desired outcomes, strategic choices, candidate initiatives and dependencies. This portfolio uses the same roadmap discipline across four different product domains.

## Reusable roadmap structure

| Horizon | Desired outcome | Candidate initiatives | Dependencies | Evidence / open questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Now | Resolve the highest-value validated problem | Focused discovery and delivery | Team capacity, data, design, engineering | Baseline and user evidence |
| Next | Improve adoption, reliability or operational performance | Workflow improvement and integration hardening | Platform / partner dependencies | Usage and quality signals |
| Later | Expand product value or scale | New capabilities, optimisation or automation | Strategy, investment and risk review | Validated opportunity |

## Applying roadmap thinking across the four product domains

| Product domain | Example outcome areas | Candidate roadmap themes |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate Travel Management | Traveller experience, compliant travel, reliable booking, spend visibility | Policy workflows, trip management, supplier integrations, mobile experience, spend intelligence |
| B2B & B2C Travel Technology | Conversion, channel adoption, supplier quality, package completion | Search and merchandising, supplier connectivity, pricing, booking and post-booking servicing |
| Healthcare Systems | Workflow efficiency, interoperability, reliable patient / laboratory information | HIMS / EHR workflows, LIS connectivity, device interfacing, result validation and auditability |
| Vulnerability & Incident Management | Exposure visibility, actionable response, remediation effectiveness | Scanner coverage, finding quality, asset enrichment, incident lifecycle, correlation and analytics |

These are example outcome areas, not commitments or assertions about a particular release plan. Validate each roadmap with evidence, product strategy and team constraints.

## Roadmap hygiene

- Prioritise outcomes, not a list of promised features.
- Show confidence, assumptions and dependencies.
- Explain why work is included and what is excluded.
- Consider user value, business impact, risk, effort and operational readiness.
- Separate discovery bets from delivery commitments.
- Update the roadmap when evidence changes.
